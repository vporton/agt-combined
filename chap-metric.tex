\chapter{Metric spaces}

The category of \emph{most general nonnegative real metrics} (MGNRMs)
has sets as objects and all functions
$\rho:X\times Y\to[0,+\infty]$ as arrows from $X$ to $Y$. Composition
with $\sigma:Y\to Z$ is given by
\[
 (\sigma\circ\rho)(x,z)=\inf_{y\in Y}\bigl(\rho(x,y)+\sigma(y,z)\bigr).
\]
The infimum of the empty set is $+\infty$. The identity on $X$ is zero
on the diagonal and $+\infty$ elsewhere. Order each hom-set by reverse
pointwise order.

\begin{proof}
Addition by any fixed element of $[0,+\infty]$ preserves infima.
Thus, for $\tau:Z\to W$, either parenthesization of the triple composite
has value
\[
 \inf_{y\in Y,\,z\in Z}
 \bigl(\rho(x,y)+\sigma(y,z)+\tau(z,w)\bigr).
\]
The diagonal term proves both identity laws. Addition and infimum
are monotone, so composition is monotone in the stated order.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathbf{1}$ denote a one-point set. Postcomposition on
$\Hom(\mathbf{1},X)$ gives an ordered semicategory action. In terms of
functions $u:X\to[0,+\infty]$ with reverse pointwise order, its action is
\[
 (\supfun{\rho}u)(y)=\inf_{x\in X}\bigl(u(x)+\rho(x,y)\bigr).
\]
Functoriality and monotonicity follow from the ordered composition
just proved. To recover $\rho(x,y)$, apply the action to the function
that is zero at $x$ and $+\infty$ elsewhere and evaluate at $y$.
Thus the matrices themselves are interspaces, with a faithful action.

\begin{defn}
For subsets $A\subseteq X$ and $B\subseteq Y$, extend the notation by
\[ \rho(A,B)=\inf_{x\in A,\,y\in B}\rho(x,y). \]
\end{defn}

\begin{rem}
This is well-defined thanks to MGNRM being nonnegative and allowing the infinite value.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}
~
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\rho(I\cup J,Y) = \min\{\rho(I,Y), \rho(J,Y)\}$;
\item $\rho(X,I\cup J) = \min\{\rho(X,I), \rho(X,J)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
We prove the first. The second follows by the same splitting of the infimum:
\begin{multline*}
\rho(I\cup J,Y) = \\
\inf_{x\in I\cup J,y\in Y}\rho(x,y) = \\
\min\left\{\inf_{x\in I,y\in Y}\rho(x,y), \inf_{x\in J,y\in Y}\rho(x,y)\right\} = \\
\min\{\rho(I,Y), \rho(J,Y)\}.
\end{multline*}
\end{proof}

Let~$a$ be a most general metric. I denote~$\Delta_a$ the funcoid determined by the formula
\[
X\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \Leftrightarrow \rho_a(X,Y)=0.
\]

(If~$a$ is a metric, then it's the proximity induced by it.)

Let's prove it really defines a funcoid:

\begin{proof}
Not $\emptyset\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y$ and not $X\rsuprel{\Delta_a}\emptyset$ because \[ \rho_{a}(\emptyset,Y)=\rho_{a}(X,\emptyset)=+\infty. \]

The same infimum argument applies in each variable. For the first,
\[ (I\cup J)\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \Leftrightarrow
I\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \lor J\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y. \]
Really,
\begin{multline*}
(I\cup J)\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \Leftrightarrow \\
\rho_a(I\cup J,Y)=0 \Leftrightarrow \\
\min\{\rho_a(I,Y),\rho_a(J,Y)\}=0 \Leftrightarrow \\
\rho_a(I,Y)=0\lor\rho_a(J,Y)=0 \Leftrightarrow \\
I\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \lor J\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y.
\end{multline*}
\end{proof}

\begin{obvious}
\begin{multline*}
X\rsuprel{\Delta_a}Y \Leftrightarrow \\
\forall\epsilon>0\exists x\in X,y\in Y:
\lvert\rho_a(x,y)\rvert<\epsilon.
\end{multline*}
\end{obvious}

\begin{thm}
\[ \supfun{\Delta_a}X = \bigsqcap_{\epsilon>0}\bigcup_{x\in X}B(x,\epsilon) \]
($B(x,\epsilon)$ is the open ball of the radius~$\epsilon$ centered at~$x$).
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
\begin{multline*}
Y\nasymp\supfun{\Delta_a}X\Leftrightarrow X\suprel{\Delta_a}Y \Leftrightarrow \\ \forall\epsilon>0\exists x\in X,y\in Y:\rho_a(x,y)<\epsilon.
\end{multline*}
\begin{multline*}
Y\nasymp\bigsqcap_{\epsilon>0}\bigcup_{x\in X}B_a(x,\epsilon) \Leftrightarrow \\ \forall\epsilon>0:Y\nasymp\bigcup_{x\in X}B_a(x,\epsilon) \Leftrightarrow \\
\forall\epsilon>0\exists x\in X:Y\nasymp B_a(x,\epsilon) \Leftrightarrow \\
\forall\epsilon>0\exists x\in X,y\in Y:\rho_a(x,y)<\epsilon.
\end{multline*}
\end{proof}

The funcoids also give an action on filters, by
$\rho\mapsto\supfun{\Delta_\rho}$. Its functoriality follows from the
following compatibility.

\begin{lem}
$\Delta_{b\circ a}=\Delta_b\circ\Delta_a$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
Let $a:X\to Y$, $b:Y\to Z$, $A\subseteq X$, and $C\subseteq Z$.
By funcoid duality, $A\rsuprel{\Delta_b\circ\Delta_a} C$ holds if and
only if the filters $\supfun{\Delta_a}A$ and
$\supfun{\Delta_b^{-1}}C$ intersect. The displayed formula for
$\Delta_a$ gives the former a basis consisting of the sets of
$y$ that satisfy $a(x,y)<\epsilon$ for some $x\in A$.
The latter has the basis consisting of the sets of $y$ that satisfy
$b(y,z)<\epsilon$ for some $z\in C$. The two bases are decreasing as
$\epsilon$ decreases, so the filters intersect exactly when the two
basis sets intersect for every common $\epsilon>0$.

This is equivalent to
$\inf_{x\in A,y\in Y,z\in C}(a(x,y)+b(y,z))=0$.
Indeed, a sum less than $\epsilon$ makes both terms less than
$\epsilon$. Conversely, applying the basis condition with
$\epsilon/2$ makes the sum less than $\epsilon$.
This is precisely $A\rsuprel{\Delta_{b\circ a}}C$.
The funcoids agree because their relations on subsets determine them.
\end{proof}

Each funcoid action is monotone on filters. If $a\sqsubseteq b$, then
$a(x,y)\ge b(x,y)$ at every pair, so every $(A,C)$ related by
$\Delta_a$ is related by $\Delta_b$. This makes the action on filters
monotone in the matrix as well.

\fxnote{The above can be generalized for the values of the metric to be certain ordered additive semigroups instead of nonnegative real numbers.}

\section{Functions as metrics}

We want to consider functions in relations with MGNRM. So we we will consider (not only functions but also) every morphism~$f$ of category~$\mathbf{Rel}$ as an MGNRM by the formulas $\rho_{f}(x,y) = 0$ if $x\mathrel{f}y$ and $\rho_{f}(x,y) = +\infty$ if not $x\mathrel{f}y$.

\begin{thm}
If~$\rho$ is a MGNRM and~$f$ is a binary relation composable with it, then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(\rho\circ f)(X,Y) = \rho(\rsupfun{f}X,Y)$;
\item $(f\circ\rho)(X,Y) = \rho(X,\rsupfun{f^{-1}}Y)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}

\begin{proof}
In the first composite, a term can contribute a finite value only
when $x\mathrel{f}t$. Taking the infimum over $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$
therefore gives
$\inf_{t\in\rsupfun{f}X,\,y\in Y}\rho(t,y)$.
In the second composite the condition $t\mathrel{f}y$ gives
$\inf_{x\in X,\,t\in\rsupfun{f^{-1}}Y}\rho(x,t)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Contractions}

What are (generalized) continuous functions between metric spaces?

Let~$f$ be a function, $\mu$~and~$\nu$ be MGNRMs. Provided that they are composable, what does the formula of generalized continuity $f\circ\mu\sqsubseteq\nu\circ f$ mean?

Transforming the formula equivalently, we get:

\begin{align*}
\forall x,z: (f\circ\mu)(x,z)\sqsupseteq(\nu\circ f)(x,z);\\
\forall x,z: \mu(\{x\},\rsupfun{f^{-1}}\{z\})\sqsupseteq\nu(fx,z);\\
\forall x,z,y\in\rsupfun{f^{-1}}\{z\}: \mu(x,y)\sqsupseteq\nu(fx,z);\\
\forall x,y: \mu(x,y)\sqsupseteq\nu(fx,fy).
\end{align*}

So generalized continuous functions for metric spaces is what is called \emph{contractions} that is functions that decrease distance.

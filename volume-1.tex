\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Introduced several new axiomatic systems, that are not less general than group theory.
See~\cite{important} for an explanation why this theory is super-important.}

This book is about ordered semigroup actions (OSAs) and the resulting
notion of \emph{space-in-general}. An ordered semigroup acting
monotonically on a poset has space elements, and the underlying arrows
of an ordered semicategory action are called \emph{interspaces}.
This language makes continuity and other topological notions algebraic.
The book proves that every small category is isomorphic to a full
subcategory of the category of ordered semicategory actions, and also
of its arrow category. A classical structure equipped with such an
arrow-category representation is a categorical space-in-general.
The catalogue includes graphs, topological and geometric spaces, and
ordinary algebraic structures, with the ambient size made explicit.

The principal examples developed in this volume are funcoids and reloids.
I introduce and study in detail the concepts of funcoids
which generalize proximity spaces and reloids which generalize uniform
spaces, and generalizations thereof. The concept of funcoid is generalized
concept of proximity, the concept of reloid is cleared from superfluous
details (generalized) concept of uniformity. 

Also funcoids and reloids are generalizations of binary relations
whose domains and ranges are filters (instead of sets). Also funcoids
and reloids can be considered as a generalization of (oriented) graphs,
this provides us with a common generalization of calculus and discrete
mathematics.

The concept of continuity is defined by an algebraic formula (instead
of old messy epsilon-delta notation) for arbitrary morphisms (including
funcoids and reloids) of a partially ordered category. In one formula
continuity, proximity continuity, and uniform continuity are generalized.

Also I define connectedness for funcoids and reloids.

Then I consider generalizations of funcoids: pointfree funcoids and
generalization of pointfree funcoids: staroids and multifuncoids.
Also I define several kinds of products of funcoids and other morphisms.

The book develops continuity, limit, openness, closedness, Hausdorffness,
compactness, and related notions for spaces-in-general.  Thus its subject is
not merely a collection of generalizations of proximity and uniform spaces:
it is general topology formulated as the algebra of OSAs.

Before going to topology, this book studies properties of co-brouwerian
lattices and filters.
\end{abstract}
